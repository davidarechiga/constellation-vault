## Bottom line

After the business is selected, the class project is to form a team and develop the idea into a realistic startup. The team must research the opportunity, explain how the company would operate, model its finances, and pitch it to classmates and the instructor as potential investors.

The final submission has **three separate deliverables**:

1. A written business plan
2. A financial spreadsheet
3. An investor-pitch presentation

Canvas says the **executive summary, financial statements, presentation, and two marketing sections together account for 70% of the available points**. Those areas should receive the most attention.

## Team and business selection

- Students rank two instructor-provided business ideas in the Canvas discussion.
- The instructor forms teams from students who choose the same idea.
- A student may propose a different business only if two other teammates agree to join and the instructor approves the proposal before work begins.
- If proposing an original idea, the discussion response should list that idea plus one instructor-provided option as a backup.
- Before beginning detailed work, confirm the final business, team members, each member's responsibilities, and the team's internal deadlines.

## Deliverable 1: Written business plan

### Format

- Submit as Microsoft Word or a PDF exported from Google Docs.
- The detailed template says **no more than 10 pages**, including the cover page and table of contents.
- Use 10–12 point font and no more than 1.5 line spacing.
- Number all pages and include page numbers in the table of contents.
- The separate financial spreadsheet and presentation are not included in the written-plan page count.
- Use APA-style citations for researched information.

### Cover page

The cover should say **Business Plan** and include:

- Names of all team members
- Business name
- Business address: Berkeley City College, 2050 Center St., Berkeley, CA
- A fictitious business website
- A fictitious business telephone number
- Business email address
- Date the plan was completed or revised

### Table of contents

- List each major section and its starting page.
- Finish the table of contents after the plan has been assembled and paginated.

### Section I — Executive summary

Write this section last and keep it to one page or less. It should answer:

- Who is on the team?
- What business is the company in?
- Why should someone invest in the product or service?
- What is the purpose or “why” behind the business?
- What makes the business distinctive or defensible?
- How will the team make the business model work?
- How much money is required?
- What return or exchange is being offered to investors?

### Section II — Business concept and background

Include:

- A two- or three-sentence mission statement identifying the business's purpose and target customer
- The overall business concept
- Whether the business is retail, wholesale, service, manufacturing, construction, professional, import/export, or another category
- A concise description of the product or service
- A memorable, expandable business name that is easy to spell and pronounce
- Whether the business is online, physical, or hybrid
- If physical, why the proposed location is suitable
- The story behind the business idea and why the team chose it
- A short biography of every team member, including relevant skills, education, experience, and connection to the idea

### Section III — Product or service

Explain:

- Exactly what the company will sell
- Important features, quality level, customer service, guarantees, and intended price point
- The customer need or problem addressed
- The **unique selling proposition (USP)**: why customers should choose this company instead of alternatives
- The current development status of the product or service
- What is required to become market-ready
- The production or service-delivery process
- Needed technology, equipment, suppliers, manufacturing, testing, hosting, facilities, or other resources
- The price and how it was calculated
- Whether the company is positioned as premium, mid-market, low-cost, or another clear category

### Section IV — Legal structure, management, and operations

Include:

- The chosen legal structure and why it fits the business
- Ownership percentages and decision-making control
- Whether equity will be offered to investors
- Each team member's management role, responsibilities, and expertise
- Key business functions such as accounting, accounts payable/receivable, human resources, production, quality assurance, marketing, sales, distribution, and customer support
- Positions that would need to be hired or contracted
- The skills required for those positions
- A realistic explanation of how the business will operate day to day

### Section V — Marketing plan

#### Industry description

- Identify the appropriate North American Industry Classification System (**NAICS**) code.
- Describe the industry, current conditions, major trends, and expected future direction.
- Explain where the proposed business fits within the industry.

#### Target customers

Define the target audience precisely:

- Who are they?
- Where are they located?
- What do they need?
- Are they more sensitive to price, quality, convenience, trust, or another factor?
- How do they make buying decisions?
- Where do they currently buy?
- How will the company reach them?

#### Market size and trends

- Estimate the total available market and the specific segment the company will target.
- Use credible numerical evidence and cite the source.
- Explain relevant growth trends.
- State reasonable assumptions about market penetration.

#### Competition

- Identify direct and indirect competitors.
- Compare price, quality, service, speed, convenience, and product features.
- Explain how the business expects to win customers from existing alternatives.

#### Estimated sales

- Estimate sales in units and dollars for the next three years.
- Break the first year into quarters when appropriate.
- Add a short written justification connecting the estimates to the market research, competition, price, and target customers.

#### Marketing mix and sales strategy

Cover the four Ps:

- **Product:** What is being offered and how it is differentiated
- **Price:** What customers pay and why that price makes sense
- **Place:** Where and how the product or service is sold and delivered
- **Promotion:** How prospects will learn about and become interested in it

Also explain:

- The initial target segment and any later expansion
- How prospects will be found and educated
- Which features and benefits advertising will emphasize
- Whether marketing will be local, regional, national, or international
- Direct sales, e-commerce platforms, retail partners, delivery services, or other distribution channels
- Promotional methods such as social media, direct mail, events, advertising, packaging, public relations, brochures, or trade shows
- The costs of selling, advertising, delivery, platform fees, and fulfillment; these must also appear in the financial model

### Section VI — Financial plan and risk assessment

#### Risks

Identify meaningful risks and explain how the team would manage them. Possible risks include:

- Competitors lowering prices
- Industry growth slowing
- Costs exceeding projections
- Sales falling below projections
- A competitor releasing a better product or service
- Difficulty finding qualified employees
- Underestimating startup costs or the sales cycle
- Weak hiring decisions or an overly broad target market

#### Startup and operating requirements

Research and support the estimates for:

- Operating expenses
- Marketing and sales costs
- Administrative overhead
- Rent, utilities, supplies, insurance, software, and equipment
- Labor and contractor costs
- Product development costs
- Capital equipment and other startup purchases
- Inventory or cost of goods sold
- Delivery, distribution, and platform fees

#### Income statement

The first-year profit-and-loss model should connect:

`Units sold × price per unit = revenue`

Then account for cost of goods/services and operating expenses to show projected profit or loss. The numbers used in the written plan, spreadsheet, and presentation must agree.

## Deliverable 2: Financial spreadsheet

Use the instructor's Excel template. Canvas describes a single spreadsheet containing at least:

- Estimated startup costs
- First-year income statement

The detailed business-plan template also expects support for:

- Operating expenses
- Capital requirements
- Cost of goods or service delivery
- Sales and revenue assumptions
- Resulting first-year profit or loss

The spreadsheet is uploaded separately from the written plan and should clearly identify the business name.

## Deliverable 3: Investor presentation

The presentation is a Shark Tank-style oral pitch supported by PowerPoint or a PDF slide deck. It should cover:

- The company and business concept
- The legal form of the business
- The product or service
- The customer problem and proposed solution
- The unique selling proposition
- The management team and key roles
- The target market
- Marketing and sales strategy
- Product price and cost
- Startup costs and total capital required
- Projected first-year revenue and profit or loss
- Major risks
- The amount requested from investors
- What investors receive in exchange: equity, loan repayment, royalty payments, or another clearly explained arrangement

Canvas includes Guy Kawasaki's **Only Ten Slides You Need for an Investor Pitch** as a planning resource. The pitch must be concise, evidence-based, visually clear, and consistent with the written plan and financial spreadsheet.

## Recommended project sequence

Canvas provides this order but leaves the team milestone dates blank:

1. Develop the business concept.
2. Create the mission and vision and choose the legal structure.
3. Finalize the business/product/service description.
4. Develop the management plan.
5. Complete industry and market research.
6. Build the four-P marketing plan.
7. Develop the operations plan.
8. Complete the risk assessment and financial statements.
9. Write the executive summary.
10. Finish the cover page, table of contents, APA references, and presentation.
11. Perform a consistency check across all three deliverables.
12. Upload all three required files to the final Module 11 assignment.

## Recommended team division

The final division should reflect each member's strengths, but every member should understand the entire plan.

- **Project coordinator/editor:** schedule, document structure, consistency, final proofreading
- **Business and operations lead:** concept, legal structure, ownership, staffing, workflow
- **Market-research lead:** industry, NAICS code, target market, competitors, market size, citations
- **Marketing lead:** USP, price, promotion, sales, and distribution
- **Finance lead:** startup costs, assumptions, revenue, cost of goods, expenses, income statement, investor offer
- **Presentation lead:** slide design, speaking order, rehearsal, and timing

If the team is small, combine roles. Keep a shared source list and an assumptions log so the document and spreadsheet do not contradict one another.
