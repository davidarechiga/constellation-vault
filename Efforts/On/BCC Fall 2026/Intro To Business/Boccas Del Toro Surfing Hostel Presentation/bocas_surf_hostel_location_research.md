# Bocas del Toro Surf + Dive Hostel: Location Research

**Research updated:** November 16, 2026  
**Purpose:** College business-plan feasibility study.  
**Target guests:** Backpackers, beginner-to-intermediate surfers, and guests interested in recreational scuba diving.  
**Evidence policy:** Advertised prices are not transacted prices; rates without original publication dates are marked *undated*; judgments are identified as analysis. No property has been legally or technically vetted.

## Executive finding

**Revised first choice: Isla Carenero**, subject to finding a viable parcel near accessible beginner-oriented surf and confirming utility, marine transport, and title. **Second choice: Bocas Town**, particularly close to the Carenero water-taxi dock, if operating simplicity and access to dive centers matter more than walking to surf. **Third: Big Creek–Paunch**, excellent for intermediate surf and road logistics but less naturally aligned with beginner lessons. This is a qualitative ranking, **not** a forecast of investment return.

**Dive-site proximity:** Bocas Dive Center reports **15+ bay dive sites** typically **5–10 minutes by boat** from its Bocas Town base; shallow reef depths commonly **5–15 m** (operator FAQ published approx. 2026). Panama Dive School states bay dive sites are within **10 minutes by boat** from its Carenero base (undated FAQ). These are operator claims, not independent timed measurements. Dive sites listed by operators include **Hospital Point, Casa Blanca, Casa Verde, Airport Point, Barco Viejo, Pandora, Lunch Box, and Manuel's Wall**. Seasonal outer sites include **Tiger Rock and Bastimentos caves**; these are not beginner default sites. Sources: https://bocasdivecenter.com/faqs/ and https://www.panamadiveschool.com/fun-dives.

**Seasonal complementarity:** Panama Dive School states diving operates year-round, with **late August–early November** especially favorable for calm seas and seasonal cave trips, and **Tiger Rock in September for advanced divers only**. Tourism Panama likewise emphasizes September–October for cave diving under suitable conditions. These are *operator/agency guidance*, not measured monthly visibility, occupancy, or cancellation data. Sources: https://www.panamadiveschool.com/faq and https://www.tourismpanama.com/places-to-visit/bocas-del-toro/things-to-do/watersports-beaches/diving-snorkeling/.

**Why scuba affects location choice:** Carenero has an established dive operator on the island **and** beginner/intermediate surf options nearby. Bocas Town has several dive shops and a simple boat connection to Carenero, making it a close second. Solarte improves for dive-specific stays because of Ankay and nearby dive sites, but lacks verified walk-to beginner surf. This is a business interpretation, not evidence of demand or profits.

**Recommended operating model (proposal, not sourced market fact):** Sell beds plus separately bookable surf lessons, board rental, Discover Scuba, certification referrals, and guided dives through established insured operators. Confirm referral commissions, pickup arrangements, equipment handling, liability, permits, certification/medical screening, and cancellation policy. Do not assume a novice can safely dive and surf on the same day without appropriate planning, fatigue management, and dive-to-flight interval guidance.

### Infrastructure and title

INEC's **2023 census**, reported by UNFPA in **December 2025**, found for **Bocas del Toro Province**: **15.2% of homes without electricity, 14.4% without potable water, 12.1% without sanitary facilities, and 53.5% with garbage collection**. **These are province-wide household figures, not site-specific island service rates.** Source: https://panama.unfpa.org/sites/default/files/pub-pdf/2025-12/17112025-MAQ-ASP-V4-LARGO-Final%20%281%29.pdf.

For every parcel, independently verify grid service, generator/solar backup, water source/storage/treatment, wastewater/septic approval, internet speeds/redundancy, trash hauling, drainage/flood elevation, coastal erosion, and safe access during storms.

**Legal caution:** Registered *titled land*, rights of possession (ROP), and maritime/coastal concessions are distinct. Do not treat an advertised title or an ROP transfer as legal proof of developability. Law 80 of 2009 and Panama Maritime Authority concession rules are relevant but not a parcel-specific zoning determination. See https://www.organojudicial.gob.pa/uploads/wp_repo/uploads/2016/11/Ley-80-de-2009.pdf and https://www.amp.gob.pa/wp-content/uploads/2023/12/Resolucion-J.D.-No.-010-2019-Aprueba-el-reglamento-de-concesiones.pdf. Obtain Public Registry/title and municipal/environmental reviews before relying on a site.

**Weather:** A November 2, 2025 Bastimentos National Marine Park closure during heavy swell/wind/rain illustrates disruption risk, but does not establish annual closure frequency. Marine transport and surf/dive trips need weather-contingency policies.

## 6. Revised ranking and trade-offs

1. **Isla Carenero — preferred:** Best apparent combination of nearby learner/progression surf, existing on-island dive center, and short town boat connection. **Risks:** water taxi dependency, land/legal feasibility, wastewater, utilities, freight. Prioritize sites near beginner-oriented breaks, **not** simply closest to advanced Carenero Point.
2. **Bocas Town — strongest operational alternative:** Multiple dive operators, walkable supplies and nightlife, airport island, and short boat trip to Carenero surf. **Risk:** cannot market walk-to beginner surf unless a particular location demonstrably provides it; competition and noise.
3. **Big Creek–Paunch:** Road-based access and intermediate surf. **Risk:** closest surf may be too advanced for first-timers; diving usually requires town/operator pickup.
4. **Bastimentos Old Bank:** Village identity and nature; access and beginner conditions less dependable.
5. **Bastimentos Red Frog/Wizard:** Beach experience, but isolation, weather and surf safety constraints.
6. **Solarte:** Compelling scuba/conservation niche, but weak verified walk-to surf.

**Decision rule:** If *walking to suitable beginner/intermediate surf* is mandatory, Carenero wins **only after a specific parcel and route are verified**. If *low operating friction* and easy dive shop access matter more, Bocas Town could win. If *intermediate surfing and road logistics* dominate, reconsider Big Creek–Paunch.

## 7. Remaining research gaps

- Actual surf-break-to-parcel walking times, access rights, tides, safety, and boat transfers to advanced breaks.
- Comparable dorm/private quotes for identical dates in peak and low seasons; room/bed counts, occupancy, ADR and RevPAR.
- Complete accommodation census including operators not on Hostelworld; exact surf/dive packages and pickup policies.
- Three or more currently available titled or legally developable properties and three lease comparables **per finalist area**.
- Parcel zoning, construction setbacks, environmental approvals, water and septic feasibility, grid reliability and flood/erosion maps.
- Verified water-taxi day/night/group/board-bag fares and cancellation frequency.
- Dive partner commission rates, commercial liability insurance, permits, dive safety and emergency protocols.
- Police-reported theft by neighborhood and evidence on noise and nighttime safety.
- Monthly surfable beginner days, diving visibility, dive cancellations, and joint demand/seasonality.
- Whether the hostel could offer reliable dive/surf bundles without conflicts in instruction schedules and sea conditions.
