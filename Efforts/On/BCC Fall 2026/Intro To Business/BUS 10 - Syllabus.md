## Course Overview
Every day we're impacted by business — products (clothes, cars, computers) and services (Uber, doctors, hair salons, Spotify). "Business" broadly includes the companies or organizations where we work, study, or invest, fueling the ~$30 trillion US economy.

US businesses operate in a dynamic global economy and must navigate differences in politics, laws, economic systems, language, currency, social and cultural norms, technology, and trade barriers.

**Course flow:**
1. The global environment via **PESTEL** (Political, Economic, Socio-Cultural, Technological, Environmental, Legal)
2. Entrepreneurship and legally forming a business
3. Key functions a business needs to run
4. Managing a business, accounting for revenue/expenses, judging profitability

**Culminating project:** build a business plan for a start-up venture and pitch it to a panel of peer investors ("Shark Tank" style).

## Course Objectives
By the end you should be able to:
1. Assess the viability of business as a personal career choice.
2. Cite the various ways the U.S. government affects, restricts, and protects business.
3. Compare and contrast advantages/disadvantages of each form of business ownership.
4. Define business ethics and explain the role of social responsibility in an organization.
5. Understand basics of business law: contracts, torts, intellectual property, the US legal system.
6. Analyze a business decision for legal, ethical, and CSR concerns.
7. Compare and contrast the primary economic systems.
8. Use key terms to explain the U.S. economic condition.
9. Understand how legal, political, socio-cultural, environmental, economic, and technology dynamics impact businesses and stakeholders.
10. Define and describe management roles: planning, organizing, leading, controlling.
11. Identify current production & operations processes, including effects on sustainability.
12. Identify key HR management functions and laws.
13. Identify the marketing mix and key tools, terms, and strategies → integrated marketing communication strategy.
14. Describe how technology impacts all primary business functions.
15. Evaluate the basic components of financial statements and ratio analysis.
16. Explain the importance of finance, types of financing, analyzing financial info, and basic financial controls.
17. Understand personal financial management principles, tools, decisions, investment options, and mechanisms.
18. Summarize components of risk management and basic insurance concepts.
19. Understand the purpose, content, and approach to developing a business plan.
20. Use business vocabulary correctly in oral and written communication; obtain and properly cite business references.

## Student Learning Outcomes (SLOs)
1. Analyze a business situation and recommend a solution or plan for improvement.
2. Analyze and apply legal and ethical principles in business decision making.
3. Obtain & properly cite business information using traditional and electronic sources.

### 2. Business Case Studies (2 × 25 = 50 pts)
1. Environmental/Business Ethics — *Something's Rotten in Hondo*
2. Marketing Ethics — *The Incredible Shrinking Potato Chip Package*

Read a one-page synopsis, then analyze **stakeholders**, **alternative solutions**, and **recommendation/justification** (each ⅓ of the grade). Submitted via a shared Discussion Forum.

### 3. Profit & Loss (Income) Statement (50 pts)
Build a P&L for **Crest Shoe Company** using the instructor's Excel template. Formulas needed for:
- Net Sales Revenue
- Total Cost of Goods Sold
- Gross Profit
- Total Marketing & Sales Expense
- Total General & Administrative Expenses
- Total Operating Expenses
- Net Income Before Taxes
- Taxes (25% of Net Income Before Taxes)
- Net Income

### 4. Strategic Planning — Personal SWOT / Goals (100 pts)
- Personal SWOT across: **education/career**, **financial**, **social/family**, **health/wellbeing**.
- Set goals/objectives for each area at **1, 3, and 5 years**.
- Template provided. Grading: applying business concepts (50%), comprehensiveness of SWOT (25%), translating SWOT into goals (25%). Submitted privately in Canvas.

### 5. Target Market Customer Profile (50 pts)
A. Pick a real product or business (local, online, or well-known brand).
B. Define its primary target customer:
- **Demographics** — age, gender, marital status, education, income
- **Psychographics** — personality, values, interests, hobbies, lifestyle
- **Behavior** — purchasing habits, shopping habits, preferred channels, brand interactions
- **Pain points & goals** related to buying the product / being a customer

C. Build the profile using the instructor's template or your own format (tables, bullets, etc.), with each element clearly defined.

### 6. Shark Tank Project: Business Plan & Investor Pitch (250 pts)
- Small-group culminating project, ~⅓ of the course grade.
- Pick an instructor-provided opportunity (or propose your own), write a comprehensive business plan, and deliver a **10–15 minute pitch** to an investor panel (slides, handouts, etc. welcome).
- Must follow the provided template/specs. Final plan submitted in Canvas with a cover page (business name, team names, date, class/section).
- ⚠️ **No extensions** on the final written plan.
- Rubric and a milestone project plan provided — complete each section as the matching topic is covered in class.
- **360° peer evaluations** factor into grades.
- Teams can "fire" a member for cause (with professor approval); that person completes a plan solo. If teammates withdraw, remaining members finish everything (grading accounts for extra scope).

### 7. Practice Quizzes (10 × 10 = 100 pts)

- Open book, **two attempts**, no time limit; higher score counts.
- Cover textbook, lectures, handouts, discussion; 1–3 chapters each, non-cumulative. Online only.

Academic dishonesty includes:

Assignments are scanned for similarity and AI-generation probability; quizzes/exams are randomized per student.
