## Chapter cheat sheet (OpenStax order)

**OS Ch 1, Economic Systems & Business [UB 1–2].**

*Business basics:* Profit = revenue − costs. More risk, more potential profit. Stakeholders are everyone affected by a business (owners, employees, customers, suppliers, community, government). The factors of production are land, labor, capital, entrepreneurship and knowledge. The business environment has economic, technological, competitive, social and global parts.

*Economics:* Scarcity drives choices. The main systems are free-market capitalism, socialism, communism, and mixed economies (like the US). Supply and demand meet at the equilibrium price. Key indicators are GDP, the unemployment rate, inflation (CPI) and productivity. The government acts through fiscal policy (taxes and spending); the Fed acts through monetary policy (money supply and interest rates).

**OS Ch 2, Ethics & Social Responsibility [UB 4].** Ethics goes beyond legality. Ethical checks: Is it legal? Is it balanced? How will I feel about it? Corporate social responsibility (CSR) means responsibility to customers, investors, employees and society, including the environment. This is the lens for the Hondo case.

**OS Ch 3, Global Marketplace [UB 3].** Comparative advantage means producing what you make most efficiently and trading for the rest. Ways into foreign markets: exporting, licensing, franchising, contract manufacturing, joint ventures, and direct investment. Trade barriers include tariffs, quotas and embargoes. Exchange rates, culture and law all shape global business.

**OS Ch 4, Forms of Business Ownership [UB 5].** Sole proprietorship (simple, but unlimited liability), partnerships (general and limited), corporations (limited liability but double taxation; S corporations avoid the double tax), LLCs (limited liability with pass-through taxes), franchises, and cooperatives.

**OS Ch 5, Entrepreneurship [UB 6].** Entrepreneurs' traits: self-directed, self-nurturing, action-oriented, highly energetic, and tolerant of uncertainty. The business plan. Funding sources: personal savings, friends and family, loans, angel investors and venture capital. The SBA (Small Business Administration). Why small businesses fail: poor management and running out of money.

**OS Ch 6, Management & Leadership [UB 7].** The four functions are planning, organizing, leading and controlling. Planning starts with vision, then mission, goals, objectives and SWOT. Leadership styles: autocratic, participative (democratic) and free-rein. Management levels: top, middle and supervisory.

**OS Appendix A, Legal & Tax Environment [UB App A].** Torts (wrongful acts such as product liability), contracts (offer, acceptance, consideration, capacity, legality), intellectual property (patents, copyrights, trademarks), the UCC, and bankruptcy (Chapters 7, 11 and 13).
