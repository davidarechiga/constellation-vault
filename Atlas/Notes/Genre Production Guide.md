# Genre Production Guide

Starting points for the styles [[Sprawling]] works in. Built from web research (Nov 13, 2026) plus general production practice. Numbers are starting points, not rules. Source quality varies: most are tutorials and magazine pieces, and emo/post-hardcore-specific advice online is thin, so those sections lean more on general rock mixing. Pair with [[Mixing and Mastering Cheat Sheet]] and [[Plugin Inventory and Best Practices]].

## Quick comparison

| Style | Tempo | Low end | Drums | Space / texture | Vocal |
|-------|-------|---------|-------|-----------------|-------|
| Electronic (house/tech) | 120-128 | Kick + bass locked, sidechained | Loop-based, tight | Filters, subtle reverb | Chopped / processed |
| UK garage | 130-135 (speed garage 138-140) | Sub answers drum gaps, mid-bass layer | Broken 2-step, swung hats | Chopped vocals, short verbs | Pitched/chopped diva vocals |
| Bass music | 140 (dub) / 170-175 (DnB) | Clean mono sub + distorted mid layer | Punchy, sparse | Wide mids/highs, mono lows | Chops, effects |
| Shoegaze | Mid-tempo | Warm, woolly bass | Soft, washed | Layered fuzz, reverb, modulation | Buried, part of the wash |
| Pop-punk | 160-200 | Bass carries the bottom, kick = beater click | Loud snare, tight | Dry-ish, big guitars | Upfront, hooky |
| Emo (incl. midwest) | Varied | Natural, open bass | Room-miked, dynamic | Clean chimey guitars, reverb, lo-fi | Raw, intimate |
| Post-hardcore | Varied | Tight kick + bass, DI under the amp | Heavy, punchy | Dense layered guitars | Sung + screamed layers |

## Electronic (house, tech house, techno)

- **Kick and bass first.** Leave space under the kick for the bass: bass sits lower or ducks around the kick. Sidechain compression from the kick: fast attack, quick release, enough to hear it pump but stay clean.
- **Arrangement:** the 8-bar loop is the trap. Move forward by subtracting and adding elements gradually (drop the hats, add a percussion layer) instead of big builds only.
- **Bass** holds the low-mid and stays out of the way above ~300 Hz.
- **Chopping samples:** isolate a one- or two-bar piece of a loop, repeat it, and use filters, phaser, flanger or chorus to build different sections. Slice a kick or snare from one loop and layer it on another.
- **Tools you have:** Serum 2, Vital, Arturia FX for tape/filter, Decapitator for drums, Goodhertz Lohi for mono bass.

Sources: [Attack Magazine: Raw French House](https://www.attackmagazine.com/technique/beat-dissected/raw-french-house/), [Attack Magazine: Progressive Techno](https://www.attackmagazine.com/technique/beat-dissected/progressive-techno/), [Sonarworks: creative sampling for drum loops](https://www.sonarworks.com/blog/learn/spice-up-your-drum-loops-with-creative-sampling), [Abletonic: tech house guide](https://abletonic.vercel.app/en/blog/how-to-make-tech-house-ableton).

## UK garage (2-step, 4x4, speed garage)

- **Tempo:** about 130-135 BPM; speed garage faster (~138-140).
- **2-step rhythm:** no four-on-the-floor. Kick on beat 1 and a syncopated hit (around the "and" of 2 or on 3), snare/clap on 2 and 4. Different tutorials give different step numbers because grid resolutions differ.
- **Swing is the defining feature.** Roughly 55-65% swing across the grid, or push/pull 16th hats by hand so they skip. Playing 16ths slightly late gives that shuffle.
- **Bass:** a sub that answers the gaps in the drums rather than following every kick, plus a mid-bass layer so it's audible on small speakers. Light sidechain to the kick for clarity.
- **Vocals:** chopped and pitched R&B-style vocal snippets, short reverb, and fills between drum hits.
- **Modern new wave** (Kettama, Sammy Virji, Interplanetary Criminal) is tighter and heavier on bass than the originals.
- **Mixing coverage is thin online.** Keep it tight and club-ready, crisp backbeat, mono sub.
- **Tools you have:** Logic Drummer/sampler, Kontakt/Battery for drum hits, SubCulture/Serum for sub, Decapitator for the bass mids, short plate (LittlePlate) for vocals.

Sources: [Reason Studios: how to make UK garage](https://www.reasonstudios.com/news/post/how-to-make-uk-garage), [Native Instruments blog](https://blog.native-instruments.com/uk-garage-music), [MusicRadar: UK garage beat](https://musicradar.com/how-to/uk-garage-tutorial), [Producer School: UKG resurgence](https://theproducerschool.com/blogs/featured-blogs/uk-garage-resurgence-how-the-new-wave-kettama-sammy-virji-interplanetary-criminal-differs-from-the-originals).

## Bass music (dubstep, DnB, bass house)

- **Two jobs, two layers:** a clean sine-ish **sub** (mono, nothing above ~100-120 Hz) and a **mid layer** with distortion, FM or wavetable grit for audibility on phones and laptops.
- **Multiband distortion:** split the bass at ~100-120 Hz, distort only the upper band, keep the sub clean. Filter extreme sub and extreme highs off the grit channel.
- **Phase:** layers can cancel. Check in mono, flip polarity or nudge timing, and high-pass the mid layer.
- **Stereo:** keep the sub centered; widen the upper layers only.
- **Dynamics:** keep drums punchy and arrangements sparse so the bass has room. Design in the synth, then EQ lightly.
- **Tools you have:** Serum 2, Massive/Massive X, FM8, Vital, SubCulture/Sub Generator, Goodhertz Midside/Lohi, Melda MRatioMB/MultiBand, Decapitator.

Sources: [MusicRadar: split your sub bass into bands](https://www.musicradar.com/tuition/tech/how-to-split-your-sub-bass-into-multiple-bands-620613), [Production Expert: sub bass tips](https://production-expert.com/production-expert-1/8-sub-bass-production-tips-to-shake-the-room?format=amp), [Dubspot: sub bass mixing](https://blog.dubspot.com/sub-bass-mixing-tips), [Nail The Mix: multiband bass distortion](https://www.nailthemix.com/?p=9193).

## Shoegaze

- **Wall of sound by stacking effects** (fuzz, reverb, delay, chorus, flanger, vibrato) or by overdubbing several guitar parts with slightly different tones.
- **Order matters:** fuzz before reverb is standard; fuzz *after* reverb gives a thicker, woollier sound and interacts with reverb tails. Reverse reverb before distortion makes the genre's swell. Shimmer (pitch-shifted) reverb gives rising tails. Sources disagree, so try both.
- **Motion:** constant modulation blurs note definition. Kevin Shields' tremolo-arm "glide" gives a broken-tape warble.
- **Mix:** dense, instruments bleed into each other, vocals often buried on purpose. Cut the lows on the guitars so the wash doesn't swamp bass and kick.
- **Tools you have:** Guitar Rig 6, Arturia Dist/Fuzz, Efx AMBIENT/REFRACT/MOTIONS, Rev INTENSITY/PLATE-140/LX-24, Delay TAPE-201/ETERNITY, chorus/flanger/phaser from Arturia, Tremolator, Melda modulation.

Sources: [iZotope: 4 ways to get a shoegaze sound](https://www.izotope.com/en/learn/4-ways-to-get-a-shoegaze-sound), [Riffhard: shoegaze guitar tone](https://www.riffhard.com/how-to-get-a-shoegaze-guitar-tone/), [Mixdown: top tips for shoegaze guitar tones](https://mixdownmag.com.au/?p=46043), [Output: shoegaze plugins](https://output.com/blog/best-shoegaze-plugins).

## Pop-punk

- **Guitars:** punchy but not swamping the vocal. High-pass around 80-120 Hz, low-pass if the top gets shrill and fights cymbals and vocal.
- **Drums:** snare is about crack in the upper mids more than weight. Fast kicks get flabby, so favor the beater click over low-end boom.
- **Bass:** carries the bottom; check punch in the ~60 Hz range and fill the 200-600 Hz range with the guitars since punk arrangements are sparse.
- **Vocals:** sit in the mix, not on top of it or buried. Doesn't need pop-level polish; it's fine to roll off above ~10 kHz. Cut competing frequencies in the guitars instead of only raising the vocal.
- **Workflow:** buses for drums, guitars, BGVs. Watch phase.
- **Don't mix it like metal.**
- **Tools you have:** Guitar Rig 6, Solid Bus Comp, Comp FET-76 on vocal/snare, Decapitator.

Sources: [Nail The Mix: pop-punk guitars (Fall Out Boy)](https://www.nailthemix.com/?p=9126), [iZotope: mixing rock](https://www.izotope.com/en/learn/6-tips-for-mixing-good-ol-fashioned-rock-and-roll), [Reverb: clarity in metal and punk mixes](https://reverb.com/news/3-tips-for-bringing-clarity-to-your-metal-and-punk-mixes), [Gearspace: pop-punk mixing thread](https://gearspace.com/threads/pop-punk-mixing.769172/).

## Emo (including midwest emo)

- **Clean, chimey guitars** are the foundation. Single-coil Tele/Strat style tone, glassy clean amps (Fender-style), reverb and delay for depth, chorus for movement. Open and alternate tunings, tapping and interlocking parts.
- **Interlocking parts:** carve space with EQ so each guitar has its own lane.
- **Drums:** room sound is central; record or mix for natural dynamics and room ambience. Sparse, dynamic playing.
- **Compression:** light. Keep dynamics.
- **Vocals:** raw and emotional, intimate. Quiet-room vocals vs. lo-fi texture are two valid approaches.
- **Lo-fi isn't a shortcut.** Raw can be harder to get than clean.
- **Tools you have:** Guitar Rig 6 (clean amp + chorus + reverb), Replika/TAPE-201 delay, Rev SPRING-636 for amp-style spring reverb, Tape J-37 for gentle tape tone, MixChecker for check.

Sources: [Riffhard: midwest emo guitar tone](https://www.riffhard.com/?p=36934), [PMT: midwest emo guitar tone guide](https://pmtonline.co.uk/blog/2022/06/22/midwest-emo-guitar-tone-guide), [Premier Guitar: American Football rig rundown](https://www.premierguitar.com/videos/rig-rundowns/american-football), [Joey Sturgis Tones: indie rock and emo tones](https://joeysturgistones.com/blogs/learn/dialing-in-indie-rock-emo-guitar-tones), [Splice: dream pop guitar tone](https://splice.com/blog/dream-pop-guitar-tone/).

## Post-hardcore

- **Guitar layering:** two players or takes, ideally with two amps each, married into one tone as the foundation. Too many rhythm guitars panned everywhere become mush. A clean DI underneath a distorted amp track keeps the low end tight.
- **High-pass:** vocals around 100 Hz and guitars around 80 Hz as starting points.
- **Screams:** layer them (quad-tracking for width) and keep them narrow/near-mono when the guitars are busy so the guitars own the stereo field.
- **Dynamics:** don't keep everything at max level. Drop guitars out or play muted chords in a bridge, let synths or clean parts take over, then bring everything back for impact.
- **Vocals:** sung and screamed sections need different treatment: compress and saturate the screams, keep sung parts open and wide with doubles and harmonies.
- **Gap:** I found little post-hardcore-specific mixing writing beyond metalcore-leaning sessions. Treat this as general loud-rock advice and trust your ears.
- **Tools you have:** Guitar Rig 6, Decapitator, Comp FET-76 (parallel on vocals), multiband tools (Melda MRatioMB), VocAlign 6 Pro for tight doubles.

Sources: [Nail The Mix: layering screams (The Contortionist)](https://nailthemix.com/lessard-contortionist-layering-screams), [Nail The Mix: Dayseeker "Sleeptalk" breakdown](https://www.nailthemix.com/?p=9485), [Nail The Mix: Lorna Shore vocals](https://www.nailthemix.com/?p=9362), [Premier Guitar: Manchester Orchestra](https://www.premierguitar.com/gear/manchester-orchestra-sharing-a-lung?page=1), [Nail The Mix: Kurt Ballou guitar tones](https://www.nailthemix.com/kurt-ballou-converge-guitar-tones).

## Genres that cross

- Shoegaze and emo share clean-to-wash guitars; shoegaze just adds more fuzz/reverb and buries the vocal further.
- UK garage and bass music share a mono-sub approach and sparse drums.
- Pop-punk and post-hardcore share bus structure; post-hardcore pushes density and dynamics harder.
