# Post-Hardcore EP Production Blueprint

Dave's cheat sheet for writing and recording the new EP, plus the expansion work: bar math, drum grids, voicing tables, a session template and a tracking-to-mix checklist. It pairs with [[Mixing and Mastering Guide]], which covers the mix and master workflow. This note covers everything before and around it: writing, arranging, tracking.

**Influences (Dave's list):** The Snake The Cross The Crown, Brand New, Name Taken, Jettie, Radiohead, Copeland.

**How to read this note:** the numbered sections keep Dave's original cheat sheet intact, then add an **Expanded** layer under each. Numbers are starting points; trust your ears. Anything I couldn't check is collected in [[#Check these before you trust them]].

## The sound in one paragraph

Two guitars playing two different jobs (crunch and chords on the left, bright arpeggios and dissonance on the right), a drummer who flips between a slow, heavy half-time feel and a fast double-time feel at the same tempo, a bass with real midrange grit, and a vocalist who moves between fragile and strained. The drama comes from **contrast**: quiet to loud, sparse to wide, half-time to double-time. Every decision below serves that contrast.

## 1. Tempo, feel and arrangement blueprint

### Dave's cheat sheet

- **Session tempo:** lock the project grid between 150 and 160 BPM. 155 BPM is the sweet spot.
- **The half-time / double-time flip:** write verses and atmospheric intros with the snare on **beat 3** (a ~77.5 BPM feel). Then move the snare to **beats 2 and 4** (a ~155 BPM feel) for choruses and climaxes.
- **Song arc (3.5 to 5 minutes):**
  1. **Intro / Verse 1:** clean or edge-of-breakup octaves or arpeggios, with the half-time snare on beat 3.
  2. **Pre-chorus:** snare build or tom-heavy linear fill into a 1-beat pause.
  3. **Chorus:** double-time snare (beats 2 and 4), wide power chords, open hi-hat and crash wash.
  4. **Bridge / breakdown:** drop back to half-time with ride bell accents, build with distorted bass and feedback into a final double-time outro.
- **Odd meter accents:** slip in an occasional 3/4 or 6/8 bar at the end of a 4-bar phrase for off-kilter, dramatic post-hardcore tension, then resolve back to 4/4.

### Expanded

**The tempo never changes, only the snare moves.** Leave the DAW tempo at 155 the whole song. The "tempo change" is a drum pattern change, which is why it works in a live room and why the click never has to move.

| Project tempo | Half-time feel | One bar (4/4) | 4 bars |
|---------------|----------------|---------------|--------|
| 150 BPM | 75 | 1.60 s | 6.4 s |
| 155 BPM | 77.5 | 1.55 s | 6.2 s |
| 160 BPM | 80 | 1.50 s | 6.0 s |

At 155, 3.5 minutes is about 135 bars and 5 minutes is about 194 bars. Use that to size sections before you write anything.

**The two grooves (16th-note grid, one bar, `x` = hit).** Starting patterns, not rules.

```
Half-time (verse)
step    1 . . . 2 . . . 3 . . . 4 . . .
Hat     x . x . x . x . x . x . x . x .
Snare   . . . . . . . . x . . . . . . .
Kick    x . . . . . x . . . x . . . . .

Double-time (chorus)
step    1 . . . 2 . . . 3 . . . 4 . . .
Hat     x . x . x . x . x . x . x . x .
Snare   . . . . x . . . . . . . x . . .
Kick    x . . . . . . . x . x . . . . .
```

In the chorus, move the hat to the **open hat or crash** and let it wash. The kick pattern barely changes; that sameness is what makes the snare move feel like the floor dropping out.

**Song map at 155 BPM (a 3:30 starting point, 136 bars).** Stretch it with a second bridge pass or a longer final chorus to reach 4:30 or more.

| Section | Bars | Feel | Guitar 1 (L) | Guitar 2 (R) | Notes |
|---------|------|------|--------------|--------------|-------|
| Intro | 8 | Half-time | Clean octaves | Quiet arpeggio | Establish the riff; hats or toms only |
| Verse 1 | 16 | Half-time | Edge of breakup | Arpeggio, high register | Single-tracked intimate vocal |
| Pre-chorus | 4 | Build | Palm-mute chugs | Tremolo lines | Snare build, tom fill, **1-beat pause** |
| Chorus 1 | 16 | Double-time | Open power chords, sus2/add9 | Melody over the top | Double-tracked vocal, room mic up |
| Verse 2 | 16 | Half-time | Same riff, one change | Add a counter-line | New element each pass (bass moves, hats open) |
| Pre-chorus | 4 | Build | | | Same pause, bigger fill |
| Chorus 2 | 16 | Double-time | | | Add harmonies |
| Bridge | 16 | Half-time | Clean swell, tape delay | Lead line | Ride bell, distorted bass enters |
| Breakdown | 8 | Half-time | Heaviest riff of the song | Feedback | Drop everything for 1 beat before it |
| Final chorus | 24 | Double-time | Everything | Everything | Gang vocals, longest chorus |
| Outro | 8 | Fade to half-time | Ring out | Ring out | Last chord decays with feedback |

**Odd meter without getting lost.** A 3/4 bar or a 6/8 bar both last **3 quarter notes**, so each one shortens the phrase by one beat (about 1.55 s at 155). The difference is feel: 3/4 is three even beats, 6/8 is two groups of three. Rules that keep it musical:
- Put it at the **end** of a 4-bar phrase (bars 1 to 3 normal, bar 4 short), so the next section arrives a beat early.
- Do it **once or twice per song**, not every phrase. It's a surprise, not a time signature.
- Add the time signature change in the DAW's arrangement so the grid and click follow it, then write the drum fill around the short bar.

**Dynamic map.** Plot energy per section on a scale of 1 to 10 before you write. A flat line of 7s is what makes a song feel like one idea. Aim for: intro 2, verse 3, pre-chorus 6, chorus 8, verse 2 4, chorus 2 9, bridge 3, breakdown 8, final chorus 10, outro 2.

## 2. Guitar and bass recipes

### Dave's cheat sheet

- **Dual-guitar interplay (the core sound):**
  - **Guitar 1 (left, 80 to 100%):** high-mid crunch (Marshall JCM800, Mesa Rectifier, or a pushed Vox AC30) playing open power chords, suspended chords (`sus2`, `add9`) and octave slides.
  - **Guitar 2 (right, 80 to 100%):** brighter, cleaner or edge-of-breakup tone (Fender Twin, AC30) playing high-register arpeggios, dissonance and tremolo-picked melodies over the chords.
- **Tuning and voicings:** Standard (E Standard) or Drop D, or a half step down (D♯ / Drop C♯) for extra warmth. Use open drones (open B and high E strings ringing over fretted bass notes) for emotional lift.
- **Effects chain:** keep delays and reverbs pre-amp or subtle on rhythm tracks so fast palm-mutes stay tight. Save lush tape delay and spring or plate reverb for clean swells and bridge leads.
- **Bass tone:** picked bass (P-Bass or Jazz Bass) with 1 to 2 kHz midrange grit (SansAmp, Darkglass or ProCo RAT blended in parallel) so it cuts through dense guitars during octave sections.

### Expanded

**Tuning names, so there's no confusion.**

| Name | Strings (low to high) | Why use it |
|------|-----------------------|------------|
| E Standard | E A D G B E | Open-string drones and the classic chime |
| Drop D | D A D G B E | One-finger power chords, heavier low end |
| Eb Standard (D♯) | E♭ A♭ D♭ G♭ B♭ E♭ | Same shapes, warmer, easier on the voice |
| Drop C♯ (half step down Drop D) | C♯ G♯ C♯ F♯ A♯ D♯ | Heavy and warm together |

When you go below E standard, move to a slightly heavier string set (about 11 to 49) and re-set the intonation, or the low strings go floppy and the palm-mutes lose their snap. Write the whole EP in one tuning where you can; switching mid-session costs time and tone.

**Open-drone cheat table (standard tuning).** The trick is to pick a key where the open **B** and open high **E** are chord tones, then keep them ringing while you move on the lower strings.

| Key | Open E is the... | Open B is the... | Flavor |
|-----|------------------|------------------|--------|
| E | root | 5th | Big, open, stable |
| A (Asus2) | 5th | 9th (the "2") | Wide, suspended |
| D (Dsus2) | 9th | 6th | Floating, unresolved |
| G | 13th/6th | 3rd | Warm, folky |
| C (Cadd9) | 3rd | 7th (maj7) | Pretty, melancholy |

Chord shapes that already do this (fret numbers low to high, `x` = muted):

| Chord | Shape | Notes |
|-------|-------|-------|
| Asus2 | x02200 | The standard open drone chord |
| Dsus2 | xx0230 | Open E rings on top |
| Eadd9 | 024100 | Big, ringing E |
| Cadd9 | x32030 | Pretty, open |

**Octave shapes (Brand New-style single-note riffs).** Root on the A or low E string, octave two strings up, and lightly mute the string in between. Slides between octaves are the "octave slides" in the cheat sheet. Use them in verses on Guitar 1 while Guitar 2 plays the arpeggio.

**Part-writing: give each guitar a job.** If both play the same thing, you've written one guitar. Rules:
- **Guitar 1** owns the **bottom and middle**: root-based power chords and octaves. Rhythm first.
- **Guitar 2** owns the **top**: arpeggios, tremolo-picked lines, a countermelody, dissonant notes (the 2nd, 4th or b9 against the chord). It should sound different from Guitar 1 even if you muted one.
- When Guitar 2 plays a dissonant note, **resolve it** within a beat or two. The tension and release is the genre.
- Leave **space for the vocal.** Where the vocal sings, thin one of the guitars out.

**Quad-track the rhythm guitars.** For every distorted rhythm part, record it **twice** on each side (a real second take, not a copy-paste). Pan the pair hard L and hard R, or use the L/R guitar split above and quad-track the parts for the chorus. That gives you the wall of width. Keep the clean arpeggios and leads to one or two takes so they stay clear.

**Amp starting points (set by ear).** Most players use too much gain.
- Use **less gain than you think** (about 40 to 50% on a Rectifier or JCM800 style amp). More gain makes chords thinner and pushes the pick attack out of the sound.
- Set the amp so a single power chord still has a clear note on each string. If it sounds like fuzz, drop the gain.
- Clean Twin or AC30: just on the edge of breakup, where a hard pick pushes it over and a soft pick cleans up. That response is the point.

**Effects order (how to reconcile the cheat sheet).** For the distorted rhythm sound, keep the amp **dry** or add time effects **after** the amp (effects loop or DAW). Pre-amp delay and reverb get distorted and smear the palm-mutes. Save the lush delay and reverb for clean swells and bridge leads, where it's the point. See [[#Check these before you trust them]].

**Bass.**
- Play with a **pick** to match the guitar attack. Double the root of the Guitar 1 power chords in choruses; move to an independent line in verses.
- **Parallel grit:** keep a clean DI (low end) and a driven amp or pedal copy (the 1 to 2 kHz bite), then blend. High-pass the driven copy around 150 to 200 Hz so it doesn't fight the kick.
- In the bridge and breakdown, switch the bass to the **fully distorted** channel for the "distorted bass" build.
- Keep everything below about 120 Hz **mono**.

**Optional fifth layer: one non-guitar texture per song.** Copeland and Radiohead both lean on something that isn't a guitar: piano, a pad, a synth swell, strings. One per song, in the verse or bridge, adds depth without clutter. Skip it if the guitars are already doing the job.

## 3. Drum programming and mixing (Circles Drums)

### Dave's cheat sheet

- **Kit selection:** **2000** for high-energy driving tracks, **Indi** for raw, looser dynamics, **Dead Vol 3** for tight, dry verse control.
- **Velocity humanization:**
  - Ghost notes and verse hats: 50 to 75
  - Verse snare (beat 3): 95 to 110
  - Chorus snare (beats 2 and 4) and crashes: 115 to 127
- **Signature fills:** kick-snare-tom linear triplets (e.g. `K - S - T1 - T2 - K - Crash`) and flammed snare hits right before chorus drops.
- **Mix automation:** keep Circles' room mic fader lower on the half-time verses for intimacy, and automate it **+3 to +5 dB** up on the double-time choruses for explosive width.

### Expanded

**Match the kit to the section, not the whole song.** If you can load more than one kit, or blend two, try **Dead Vol 3** (tight) for verses and **2000** (big) for choruses. Even if you stay with one kit, the room automation below does the same job.

**Velocity map.**

| Element | Velocity | Notes |
|---------|----------|-------|
| Ghost notes | 50 to 75 | Keep them under the snare so they're felt, not heard |
| Verse hats | 50 to 75 | Alternate 60 / 75 on 8ths so it doesn't machine-gun |
| Verse snare (beat 3) | 95 to 110 | Slightly loose, not 127 |
| Chorus snare (2 and 4) | 115 to 127 | Vary by 3 to 5 so no two hits are identical |
| Crashes | 115 to 127 | Always on the 1 of the first bar of a chorus |

**Humanize beyond velocity.** Real drummers also drift in time. Nudge the snare **5 to 15 ms late** in the half-time verses (relaxed) and keep it dead on the grid in the chorus (driving). Nudge the hats a hair ahead or behind a few notes per bar. Don't quantize to 100%; leave 70 to 80% so the energy moves.

**Fill vocabulary.** Write 3 to 4 fills and reuse them:
1. **Pre-chorus build:** snare roll or 16th-note snare, getting louder, ending in a **1-beat silence** (this is the "pause" in the arc).
2. **Linear triplet:** `K - S - T1 - T2 - K - Crash`, the cheat sheet fill. One drum at a time, no two hits together. It sounds busy but stays clear.
3. **Flam into the chorus:** a flammed snare (two hits a few ms apart) on the last beat before the chorus, with the crash and the kick landing on the 1.
4. **Floor-tom breakdown:** quarter-note floor tom with ride bell for the bridge and breakdown.

**Ride bell.** Use it in the bridge and breakdown (half-time, hats gone). Keep bell velocity around 90 to 110 so it rings as a pitched accent instead of a click.

**Room mic automation.** Keep the room fader low on the half-time verses, then bring it up 3 to 5 dB on each chorus. Do it with a **long ramp** (a beat or two) into the chorus, not a step, so it blooms. Return it in the next verse.

**Mix starting points (a guide, not a rule).**
- **Kick:** weight around 50 to 60 Hz, click around 3 to 5 kHz. Cut a little at 300 to 400 Hz if it boxes up.
- **Snare:** body around 150 to 250 Hz, crack around 2 to 5 kHz. Gentle parallel compression for the chorus.
- **Cymbals and hats:** high-pass around 300 Hz if they're muddying the guitars.
- **Overheads and room:** compress lightly; they're what make the double-time chorus feel wide.

## 4. Vocals and lyrics

### Dave's cheat sheet

- **Vocal contrast:** vulnerable, melodic chest or mixed-voice singing in the verses, with strained, impassioned belt or shouted harmonies in the choruses (the classic Name Taken and early-2000s Deep Elm and Drive-Thru Records dynamic).
- **Double-tracking:** single-track the intimate verses. Double-track the main chorus vocals (one center, one tucked **-6 to -10 dB** behind), and pan octave and fifth harmonies **30 to 50% L/R**.
- **Vocal chain:** fast compression (1176-style, 4:1, medium attack, fast release) to control aggressive peaks, then a short slapback delay (**80 to 110 ms**) instead of heavy reverb so the lyrics stay urgent and upfront.

### Expanded

**Plan the vocal by section.**

| Section | Delivery | Tracks |
|---------|----------|--------|
| Verse | Soft, close to the mic, mixed voice | 1 (single) |
| Pre-chorus | Rising, building | 1, plus a quiet double that enters on the last bar |
| Chorus | Full chest or belt, strained at the peaks | Lead + double (-6 to -10 dB) + octave/fifth harmonies (pan 30 to 50% L/R) |
| Bridge | Back to vulnerable, almost spoken | 1 |
| Final chorus | Everything, plus gang vocals | Lead, double, harmonies, **3 to 4 voices of gang vocals, each recorded twice** |

**Recording tips.**
- **Warm up**, then record the shouted and strained parts **last** in a session, or they cost you the clean takes.
- **Comp** the verse from 3 to 4 takes; **keep the chorus belt as a single full take** when you can, so the strain sounds real.
- Record the **same line twice** for a double instead of copying it. It needs the tiny timing and pitch differences to sound like a double.
- Pull the mic back a few inches for the loud parts. It controls peaks before they hit the compressor.

**Vocal chain (in order, starting points).**
1. **High-pass** around 80 to 100 Hz.
2. **Subtractive EQ**: cut any boxiness around 250 to 400 Hz or harshness around 3 to 5 kHz that bothers you.
3. **1176-style compressor**: 4:1, medium attack, fast release, about **3 to 6 dB** of gain reduction on the loud lines. (On a hardware 1176 the attack and release knobs are backwards: fully clockwise is fastest.)
4. **Second, gentler compressor** (opto style) for 2 to 3 dB of leveling.
5. **De-esser**, then **additive EQ** for air above about 10 kHz.
6. **Slapback delay**: 80 to 110 ms, feedback about 10 to 15%, low-pass the delay (around 4 to 6 kHz) so it sits behind the voice.
7. **Reverb** only on the bridge and outro, as a short plate. Heavy reverb would undo the "urgent and upfront" goal.

**Lyrics: a working method.** (This is for Dave's own writing. Nothing here writes the lyrics.)
- Pick **one image per song** and keep returning to it, rather than a list of ideas.
- Write the **chorus first**: one clear line that can be shouted by a crowd.
- Verses are **concrete and small** (a room, an object, a conversation). The chorus is the **emotion those details add up to**.
- The **bridge changes the meaning** of a line from verse 1 (same words, different feeling). It's the best way to earn the final chorus.
- Say it plain. Direct, unpoetic lines hit harder in this genre than ornate ones.

## 5. Fast recording and pre-production workflow

### Dave's cheat sheet

1. **Scratch map first:** program basic half-time / double-time drum markers in your DAW before tracking guitars, so your strumming dynamics naturally follow the tempo shifts.
2. **Track rhythm guitars to full drum energy:** even in a quiet section, play with intentional pick attack. Early-2000s post-hardcore relies on human pick noise and slight amp sag, not sterile perfection.
3. **Bus cohesion:** route all guitars to a single bus with subtle tape saturation and a **1 to 2 dB dip around 300 to 400 Hz** to leave room for the snare body and the bass guitar.

### Expanded: the full path from idea to mix

**Phase 1: Write (per song).**
- [ ] Riff and chord progression on guitar, recorded as a phone voice memo
- [ ] Section map (use the table in section 1) and a dynamic map (1 to 10)
- [ ] Chorus melody and hook line
- [ ] Scratch vocal: gibberish is fine, just melody and phrasing
- [ ] Decide the tuning and the **key** (check it fits your range; Eb tunings help)

**Phase 2: Pre-production (the scratch map).**
- [ ] Set the DAW to 155 BPM; mark sections with locators
- [ ] Program the half-time and double-time drum scratch (section 1 grids)
- [ ] Record a click-and-scratch guitar and vocal, then **listen without looking at the screen**
- [ ] Cut or fix anything that bores you. Fixing it now costs minutes, later it costs a session.

**Phase 3: Tracking order.**
1. Final drums (programmed in Circles, or recorded if you have a kit)
2. Bass (so the guitars play on top of a real low end)
3. **Rhythm guitars** (quad-tracked, to the full-energy drums)
4. Clean arpeggios and leads
5. Lead vocal, then doubles and harmonies, then gang vocals
6. Extras: piano, pad, feedback, noise

**Phase 4: Session template.**

| Group | Tracks | Color | Notes |
|-------|--------|-------|-------|
| Drums | Kick, snare, hats, toms, OH, room, crash, ride | One color | Drum bus; room fader automated |
| Bass | DI, driven, sub (if needed) | One color | Low end mono |
| Guitars L | Rhythm L (x2), clean/arp L | One color | Hard L |
| Guitars R | Rhythm R (x2), lead / dissonance R | One color | Hard R |
| Vocals | Lead, double, harmonies, gang | One color | Vocal bus |
| Texture | Piano, pad, feedback | One color | Optional |

Route all guitars to a **single guitar bus** with tape saturation and the 300 to 400 Hz dip from the cheat sheet. Use the session setup steps in [[Mixing and Mastering Guide]] (match sample rate and BPM, group and color-code, 10 ms fades, label sections).

**Phase 5: Hand off to the mix.** Follow [[Mixing and Mastering Guide]]: gain staging (kick peaks at -5 dBFS), foundation, volume-only mix, then fixes. For this genre, check the guide's genre scale for rock or alternative and use that for the kick and bass target.

## Check these before you trust them

Things I couldn't confirm from the cheat sheet or the vault. None of them block the work.

- **Pre-amp delay and reverb on rhythm guitars.** The cheat sheet says "pre-amp or subtle." Time effects placed before a distorted amp usually get distorted and smear palm-mutes, so I wrote the expanded advice as dry or after the amp. Try both and pick by ear.
- **Circles Drums details.** Kit names (2000, Indi, Dead Vol 3) and the "Room mic fader" come from the cheat sheet as written. I didn't check them against the plugin.
- **Label claims.** "Name Taken / Deep Elm / Drive-Thru" is Dave's framing. I haven't verified which bands were on which label.
- **Numbers are starting points.** EQ frequencies, compression ratios, gain percentages and delay times are typical starting values. Adjust them in the mix.
- **The influence table** has blank rows for The Snake The Cross The Crown, Name Taken and Jettie. Those need Dave's ears, not my guess.
